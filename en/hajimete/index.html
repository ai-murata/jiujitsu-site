Your First Jiu-Jitsu Tournament Guide

Everything we've told first-time competitors, every single time.

The Tournament at a Glance

  1. Entry deadlineSome tournaments close early, so watch out.
  2. Entry change period (only if you have no opponent)Within about 2 days after the deadline, choose to change your weight class or category, or withdraw. The tournament should contact you, so keep an eye on your email.
  3. Brackets releasedUsually about 2 to 3 days before the matches. Once they're out, always check them yourself.
  4. Gym match sheet sharedAfter the brackets are out, a sheet with our members' match numbers, mats and times gets shared in the tournament group chat. *But you still have to check the brackets on the official site yourself!
  5. Tournament day

3 to 1 weeks before

Check that your gi meets the tournament rulesImportant

Gi rules keep getting stricter. The gi check is only about 20 minutes before your match, so if your gi fails, you basically can't compete (it has actually happened to someone...). Check with your instructor ahead of time. You should be fine, but just in case.

(If you're cutting weight) Cut back on salt from about a week out

Drinking lots of water tends to raise your metabolism.

If you feel your base fitness slipping...

Adjust how much you train from about a week out. If your weight is fine, it's OK to just think "rest!"

RYOZO'S METHOD ─ Lessons from world champion Ryozo Murata

Your age decides how long to "do nothing" before a match.

In your 30s, do nothing for 3 days. In your 40s, 5 days. Or stick to light sparring or drills, just enough to break a sweat.

Only cut salt when you're cutting weight.

If you don't need to lose weight, keep eating salt like you normally do.

Up to the day before

No full-power training from about 2 days out

You won't recover as easily. Watch out for injuries too.

Avoid the sauna the day before (as much as you can)

It can hurt your lungs and make you gas out faster. If you're cutting weight, a half-body soak in the bath is better.

Adjust your weight with carbs (sugar) the day before (and on the day)

Protein and fiber don't work as energy. Before a match, white rice is a better pick than brown rice. It's best not to eat much wheat.

Watch out for food poisoningImportant

With raw food (like oysters), the worst case is you can't compete. Avoid it, especially the day before.

Check your match time for tomorrow one more time

We really have had a case of "I thought it was 5 p.m., but it was 10 a.m...." (they ended up missing their match). It's rare, but just in case.

RYOZO'S METHOD ─ Lessons from world champion Ryozo Murata

Bring 2 gis.

Even if you fail the gi check, with a spare you can change on the spot and still compete.

Tournament day

Eat food that's easy to digest

Especially 1 to 2 hours before, go for things that turn into energy quickly, like energy jelly drinks or yokan (sweet bean jelly). Carbs like rice balls and bread take over 3 hours to digest...

Get to the venue at least 1 to 1.5 hours before your match

Getting used to the atmosphere helps calm your nerves. Someone may have saved a block of seats, so check the tournament LINE group (LINE is a messaging app) as soon as you arrive. Venues usually have a scale anyone can use, so weigh yourself for peace of mind.

Warm up about 30 minutes before, just enough for a light sweat

Your usual basic exercises. Don't practice your best moves, your opponent might see them lol

You'll be called for weigh-in about 10 to 20 minutes before your match, and it happens right by the mat

Once you clear weigh-in, it's straight to the gi check. If you pass, you move on and wait, so bring a drink and your phone (and a light snack if you like). If you win, you'll fight again about every 15 minutes, so extra water is a great idea.

If you have a lot of matches, bring a sports drink like Pocari Sweat, not just water

It gives you energy. Good for refueling between matches.

(Especially if you come from judo) You weigh in with your gi onWatch out

Someone once found out on the day and had to do an emergency sauna 😅 In judo the custom is to weigh in without your gi, so if you come from judo, be extra careful!

(Men only) Rash guards are optional

Tell someone at your gym, "I need a corner, please!"

They'll assign the right person (someone who's finished the corner training course and doesn't have a match of their own). *Whether corners are allowed depends on the tournament, so check its rules.

Give someone your phone and ask them to film, so you can review later

Your corner, or someone watching nearby. Tournaments often post their own footage later, but you never know when.

Match Flow and Etiquette

  1. Wait until the referee calls you

    When both competitors are by the mat and the timekeeper is ready, the referee spreads both arms and waves you in.

  2. When you're called, enter and greet

    ① Bow to the mat → ② Run over to the referee → ③ Shake the referee's hand and greet → ④ Shake your opponent's hand and greet. They say it's good to greet before your opponent does (better impression, maybe?)

  3. The match starts with "Combate!"

    Most people touch hands like in regular sparring... but be careful, some will ignore yours and jump right at you (the greeting after "Combate" is manners, not a rule).

  4. When you hear "Parou!", stop

    Freeze in place, keeping your grips and legs where they are. You'll hear instructions like "Both of you, keep that position and move to the center of the mat" or "Restart from standing."

  5. After the match

    Straighten your gi, and the winner's hand is raised. Then ① shake the referee's hand and greet → ② shake your opponent's hand → ③ shake the hand of your opponent's corner, if they have one → ④ go back to where you came in and bow to the mat → ⑤ shake your own corner's hand → ⑥ (if you won) tell the timekeeper your name.
