Claude Code at a Jiu-Jitsu Gym

How we handed our gym’s back-office work to AI.

Membership fee tallies, invoices, shift schedules, live tournament results, video subtitles. Running a gym means a lot of work off the mat. Claude Code is an AI that does the work on your computer when you just talk to it. Here’s what we’ve actually used it for, laid out from White belt to Black belt. Every page on this site was made with Claude Code too.

How it helpsWHAT IT DOES

Unlike an AI that only answers in chat, it actually does the hands-on work.

Reads and makes filesIt opens Excel files, PDFs, photos, and videos, reads what’s inside, then creates new spreadsheets and documents and saves them to a folder.
Checks the webIt reads booking systems and tournament results pages and pulls out just the numbers you need.
Remembers the stepsSave your monthly routine as a “skill,” and next month a quick “Same as usual, please” runs the same steps.
A person presses the last buttonIt stops and checks before sending, paying, or deleting. You never hand over the final call.

Gym ground rulesRULES

You’ll be handling members’ money and personal information, so decide these from the start.

4 RULES

A person presses send, pay, and delete

Issuing invoices, making transfers, sending messages: Claude gets everything ready and stops. Staff press the final button.

Keep members’ names private

When publishing shift schedules or live results pages, replace staff and member names with aliases. Check the content with your own eyes before it goes public.

Don’t hand over passwords

Staff log in themselves. Claude only reads screens that are already logged in.

Keep one home for files

Save what you make in the shared folder under “year-month + project name.” Keep your desktop from getting cluttered.

How to startSTART

You can start even if nobody at your gym is good with computers. I wrote up the path I took and where I got stuck in How to Start with Claude.

  1. Install the Claude app on your gym’s computer

    Claude Code comes in the Mac and Windows desktop apps. If all your staff will use it, a team plan makes it easier to manage.

  2. Try handing it one Excel file

    Start with “Look at this file and give me this month’s total.” The first time, check for yourself that the answer is right.

  3. Pick one monthly job and make it a routine

    If it goes well, say “Turn these steps into a skill.” From next month on, one line runs the same job.

  4. Once you’re used to it, try booking systems and tournament pages

    From Purple belt on, you move to having it read screens you’re already logged in to.
