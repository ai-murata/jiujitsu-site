Get good at Claude Code without studying

2026.09.30 · Ai Murata (Manager)

Hi, it’s Ai Murata. For post number 7, I want to talk about how I get along with Claude Code.

The people who use it best don’t study

I’ll say it up front: the better people are at Claude Code, the less they’ve probably studied. They just keep doing it. That’s all.

If there’s something you don’t know, Claude will teach you all of it. So I hardly ever “study” anything.

Don’t study first and then start.
Learn as you use it.

YouTube, like the radio

Still, I do want hints like “Oh, it can do that too,” so I watch quite a lot of Claude Code videos on YouTube.

Well, not so much watch as listen while driving, like the radio. As I half-listen, something catches: “Oh, we could use that.” That level of attention feels just right.

Picking up “this could be easier” moments

I don’t really rack my brain over what to use it for. My basic rule is this: when I’m working and notice “Oh, Claude could make this easier too,” I try it.

Keep doing that, and you start finding it outside work too, in everyday life: “Wouldn’t this be so much easier if I did it this way?”

What I’m building next: a coin parking finder

For example, here’s something I want to make but haven’t yet.

Our car is a Mercedes GLE, which is on the big side. That means it won’t fit in mechanical coin parking lots (Japan’s automated pay-parking machines). When I’m going somewhere new, I look up the size limits of nearby lots before I leave. Doing that every time was a real pain.

So I’m planning to build a system like this.

The system I’m planning

  • Register your car’s size first
  • Enter where you’re going
  • It finds nearby coin parking big enough for your car
  • It also tells you which ones have a daily maximum rate

It’s no great invention. But it gets rid of one “small hassle every time.” For me, Claude Code has been that, over and over.

Talking to my computer from wake-up to bedtime

These days I open Claude as soon as I wake up and keep going back and forth with it until right before bed. I’m always talking to my computer, so Ryozo just goes “heh” and laughs at me.

But I really do feel it’s changed how I live and how I relate to work. And I think it’ll probably change even more over the next few months.

Your first step doesn’t have to be studying

If you’re thinking “To use this well, I need to study properly first,” I want to tell you there’s no need.

In today’s work or daily life, find one thing that makes you think “this is a small hassle every time,” and just tell Claude about it. Whatever you don’t understand, Claude will teach you.

I’ll keep writing about things I think will help you. Thanks for reading.
