Planning with Claude on the
15-minute drive home from the gym

2026.10.24 Ai Murata (Manager)

Just the other day, I realized I can talk to Claude in the car. With the Claude app on my phone, I can talk using just my voice, no hands.

The first thing out of my mouth was, "Whoa, so I can Claude hands-free. I had no idea." So I decided to try it out on my way home from the gym.

  • So, I want to talk now about what I need to do when I get home today.

The to-do list takes shape as you talk

I'm driving, so there's no way I can speak neatly. "So first I send the contract, right? And then after that I have to get in touch with that person too. And there's probably more to do, huh." It's just me muttering things in whatever order they come to mind.

Even so, Claude picked it all up and organized it. Then it came back with this.

  • There's also the sponsor check that's been sitting from before. Is that still open?Claude
  • Oh, that's fine, Ryozo's going to talk to them.
  • Got it. So the sponsor matter is with Ryozo. What do you need to tell that person?Claude

What surprised me was that Claude itself brought up an old to-do I'd forgotten about. And when I said "Ryozo's handling that," it took it right off my list.

It asked what I needed to tell that person, and as I went back and forth talking it through, I realized there were 3 things. By the end, it had even turned those 3 into a draft of a single message.

By the end of the 15-minute drive, my to-do list had come down to 5 or 6 items.

No more dithering once I'm home

That night I had about 4 hours of small jobs: replying on LINE, sending invoices and contracts, and so on. All of it unglamorous.

But what to do had already been decided before I got home. When I opened my laptop, I didn't have to think, "Okay, where do I start?" The drafts were ready, so all that was left was to knock it out.

Those 15 minutes of driving used to be just travel time.
Now they've become planning time.

What worked well

  • It doesn't have to be organized. Just talk in whatever order things come to you, and Claude sorts it out
  • It asks about what you've forgotten. It remembers what you talked about before and asks, "What happened with that?"
  • It separates out what's someone else's job. Say "that's Ryozo's" and it comes off your list
  • You can have the wording ready. If the message drafts are done, all you do at home is send them

Just getting rid of the time spent thinking once I sat down at my desk made my evening work so much lighter. I think I'll keep doing this on the way home from the gym for a while.
