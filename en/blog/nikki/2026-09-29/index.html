Claude and I collected 684 jiu-jitsu gyms across Japan from the map

2026.09.29 (Tue) Ai Murata (Manager)

Today’s biggest “Wait, what?” was going through Google Maps for all 47 prefectures and adding 133 new gyms to the gym guide. I also built 2 systems in one day that automatically sum up the morning news.

  1. Going through maps of all 47 prefectures: 684 gyms

    To add more gyms to the gym guide, I had Claude go through Google Maps for all 47 prefectures of Japan. It found 133 new gyms, bringing the total to 684. After that, I had it look up each gym's website, Instagram, and training schedule and add those too, so the guide now has 508 websites, 307 Instagram accounts, and 312 schedules.

    If you want to copy this, ask like this

    Starting with [prefecture], go through the map one area at a time, search for "[type of school]," and add any that aren't on the list yet, with their name and location

    Discovery Some gyms turned up on the map but couldn't be pinned to an exact spot. Out of the 684, 562 got a pin on the map. When you have Claude leave the numbers behind, you can see where the gaps are.

  2. Copying one system to make a second news page

    "Today's Jiu-Jitsu News," which I built in the morning, is a system that gathers the news every morning and summarizes only the relevant articles in Japanese. In the evening, I copied that exact system to make "Today's Claude News" too. The only change is how it picks stories: it now favors things like new model announcements and new service launches.

    If you want to copy this, ask like this

    Using the same system as the jiu-jitsu news, also make a page that summarizes [topic] news every morning. When choosing stories, prioritize [criteria]

    Discovery Instead of explaining from scratch, I just said "use the same system as that one." The news-reading bug I'd fixed that morning came along in its fixed state too.

    See what we made →

  3. Summarizing 17 papers, and rewriting one spot

    I made a page that summarizes 17 academic papers on jiu-jitsu in plain Japanese, sorted by themes like injuries, fitness, match analysis, weight cuts, and mental game. Every paper was checked against a paper database to confirm it really exists before going on the page.

    If you want to copy this, ask like this

    Find research papers on [topic] and summarize them briefly in Japanese, sorted by theme. Before adding each one, confirm that the paper really exists

    Discovery After publishing, I noticed that a one-line note about injuries said "the authors conclude this" about something the paper doesn't actually say. I changed it to "this is a common claim, not this paper's conclusion." With AI summaries, it's important to check even who's being credited with saying what.

Going through every map and hunting down papers are both things I could never get to alone. The more you think "this has got to be too much," the more you should try asking once.
