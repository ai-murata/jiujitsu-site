3 things I asked AI to do while running a tournament

2026.10.04 (Sun) Ai Murata (Manager)

Today was the day of Reversal Cup Sapporo, an SJJJF Hokkaido jiu-jitsu tournament. On a day when running the venue took everything I had, I still finished an apology notice, an analysis of past tournaments, and planning for the next tournament, all the same day. I’ve written it up so anyone running tournaments can copy it as is.

  1. If you find a mistake on the day, apologize right away

    On tournament day, we found out the medals had been made with the year "2006" instead of "2026." I immediately made an apology image for social media Stories, with the federation's logo in it and the whole medal in the shot. The first draft also included things like the venue, but all I needed to say was that the year was wrong, so I cut the rest.

    If you want to copy this, ask like this

    We made the medals with the wrong year, so make a polite apology for social media Stories. Include the federation's logo and show the whole medal

    Discovery An apology lands better when you cut what's extra rather than add what you want to say. Just by telling Claude "this part isn't needed" on each draft, I ended up with something short and direct.

  2. Looking at past participant numbers, adults and kids separately

    I handed over the Box shared link with this tournament's past registration data and had Claude analyze it. Pulling everything together from 2022 shows at a glance how adult and kids numbers have moved, and what changed once the tournament went to twice a year in 2024. The results went into a PDF, and I also made a participant numbers graph for Instagram, with brighter colors and the organizer's logo.

    If you want to copy this, ask like this

    Analyze this tournament's past data, split by adults, kids, and tournament. Put it in a PDF and also make a graph for Instagram

    Discovery The shortcut is saying up front how you want to split things. Even if some years are missing data, you can hand it over later and it'll redo the summary.

  3. For a seniors tournament, what age cutoff brings in people?

    I had Claude break down the participants in the All Japan Championship's master categories (age 30 and up) by age, combining the two organizations SJ and JB. If we held a tournament for seniors, how many people could enter at each age cutoff? It put the numbers and percentages in a table and compared them in a horizontal bar graph too.

    If you want to copy this, ask like this

    List the All Japan master category participants by age, with numbers and percentages. Also tell me the best age cutoff for a seniors tournament

    Discovery Don't stop at "make a list." Ask directly about what you need to decide, like "what's the best age to draw the line?", and you'll get a recommendation along with the table.

I also made a kids' sign and round stickers for our booth at the All Japan. On tournament days your hands tend to stop, but because I could ask for writing, images, and number crunching on the spot, I got my reflections and next plans into shape while they were still fresh.
