Is Claude at “Harvard valedictorian level”?
On professors’ toughest test, 4% to 61% in 1 year 8 months

2026.10.20 · Ai Murata (Manager)

Claude helps me with office work every day. Before I started using it, I heard people say, “Apparently Claude is at Harvard valedictorian level.”

I wondered if that was true, so I looked into it. Short answer: Anthropic (the company that makes Claude) doesn’t officially say that. But the numbers I found show it’s not much of an exaggeration either.

An ultra-hard test called “Humanity’s Last Exam”

There’s a test for measuring how smart AI is called “Humanity’s Last Exam.” About 1,000 university professors and researchers from 50 countries pooled questions they figured “AI won’t be able to solve,” making an exam of 2,500 questions. It covers more than 100 fields.

Here’s what the public sample questions look like.

Sample 1: Living things

  • How many pairs of tendons are supported by a small bone inside a hummingbird’s body? Answer with a number.

Sample 2: Ancient language

  • Translate this ancient Syrian script (Palmyrene) carved on a tomb from Roman times.

You’d have to be an expert on bird anatomy for the first one, and a scholar of ancient scripts to even touch the second. Questions like these come from every field. It’s a test where no matter how much of a genius you are, you can hardly solve anything outside your specialty.

4% to 61% in 1 year 8 months

The interesting part is how fast the score rose.

When the test came out in January 2025, Claude scored just 4%. It could solve only 4 out of 100 questions. Even the top AI at the time got just 8%.

By September 2026, that was about 60%. That’s 15 times higher in 1 year 8 months. Under rules that allow searching the web, it reaches about 70%.

Why it still can’t solve the other 40%

Even now, it can’t solve them all. Apparently there are 3 main reasons.

  • Knowledge that isn’t written down anywhere Very narrow specialist knowledge that’s barely in books or online
  • Reading fine detail in photos and diagrams It’s still weak at accurately reading images like worn letters on stone
  • Stumbling during long reasoning Get one step out of 10 wrong, and the answer is off

There’s also talk that the questions themselves may be wrong. Outside researchers have pointed out that for about 30% of the chemistry and biology questions, the “correct” answers themselves are doubtful. Even professors’ questions aren’t perfect, so a score of 100 may not be possible in the first place.

But working with it is like having a “valedictorian new hire”

On tests, it’s valedictorian level. So what’s it like to work with every day? The closest thing I can think of is “a new hire who graduated at the top of the class.”

  • It knows way more than I do about general topics like taxes, contracts, and print shop file rules
  • It checks an Excel file with hundreds of rows in minutes and finds the one row that doesn’t match
  • But it doesn’t know our house rules unless you teach it, like “We call this client this” or “This person’s amount needs checking every month”

On the other hand, once you teach it something, it keeps a note and follows it from then on. That’s where it differs from an ordinary tool.

There are also places where I feel a person still has to step in. In a draft email to someone outside the company, it once wrote “as we have requested” about something we hadn’t actually asked for yet. The wording was very polished. But it doesn’t understand your relationship with the other person, or “what hasn’t been said yet.” Reading before sending is a human’s job.

Summary

  • On professors’ ultra-hard test, Claude’s score went from 4% to about 60% in 1 year 8 months (about 70% with web search)
  • “Harvard valedictorian” isn’t an official claim, but in raw smarts alone, it’s not an exaggeration
  • Working with it is like having “a valedictorian new hire.” Teach it your house rules, and have a person read before sending

1 year 8 months ago it was 4 out of 100, and now it’s 60%. How far will it go in the next year? As someone who uses it every day, I’m excited and also a little scared.
