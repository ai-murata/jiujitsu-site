How a word processor fan like me fell for Claude Code

2026.09.27 · Ai Murata (Manager)

Hi, this is Ai Murata. For my third post, let me tell you a little story from the old days.

Why is a jiu-jitsu gym manager like me so hooked on Claude Code? When I really thought about it, this didn't start recently. I've always loved learning whatever the "new tool" of the moment was.

It began with a word processor in elementary school

I started using a word processor (a dedicated typing machine, common in Japan before home computers) in elementary school. I saved what I typed on floppy disks and printed it on thermal paper. That slick, shiny paper takes me back.

I wrote letters to friends and rambled on about whatever I was thinking. Typing was just so much fun that I kept writing whatever came to mind.

On the computer I also used Ichitaro (a Japanese word processing app), and I traded emails with my grandpa. Looking back, I think I was, for an elementary schooler, at the cutting edge of the time.

I don't get as absorbed as I did back then, but I think the feeling of "I love making things at a machine" has stayed at my roots all along.

In junior high, I made my own website

In junior high, I learned to set up my own website. I used a free website service and wrote the HTML (the way you put together what's inside a web page) myself.

As it happened, my junior high had a class where we made our own websites. I went completely overboard on my page, and my teacher and friends around me were seriously surprised. I still remember that clearly.

Looking back, I think I've always been good at putting things together.

College: cornered by Excel and PowerPoint

The next turning point was college. Once I got into the economics department, there were more and more situations where I just had to use Excel and PowerPoint.

If I couldn't use them, I couldn't finish my assignments. So I looked everything up. Every time I got stuck I looked it up, tried it, and looked it up again. Honestly, I didn't start because I liked it. I learned because I had to.

University staff: where I got "skilled"

After graduating I became university staff, and now I was using Excel, Word, and PowerPoint every day for work. Making tables, making documents, making materials. Doing it over and over, before I knew it they felt completely natural in my hands.

I think I got good enough with them that I can fairly say I became "skilled."

That era ended, and next came AI

But recently, I feel the air has changed. The era when being good at Excel and Word was your strength may have come to a close.

Just as Excel came after the word processor,
I think AI comes after Excel.

When I felt that, I thought, "Oh, it's that feeling again." It was the same as being cornered by Excel in college and looking it all up to learn. Only this time, it feels less like being cornered and more like jumping in on my own.

I got hooked probably because it's "the same as before"

Looking back, word processors, Excel, and AI have all been the same thing for me.

  • A new tool comes along
  • I look up what I don't understand, try it, and learn it
  • Once I can use it, work gets much easier and I can make more things

The difference with AI is that if you ask in your own words, the tool comes toward you. You don't need to learn Excel functions. Just say "I want a table like this" and it makes one. Things I used to spend hours looking up now take a single conversation.

Even so, I don't think my past experience went to waste. Because I know from Excel and Word things like "this kind of document would really help" and "this part is tough by hand," I can immediately think of what to ask AI for. All that time learning tools is now paying off in how I ask.

Learning new tools, all over again

The elementary schooler writing letters on a word processor became the college student cornered by Excel, then the university staffer with Word, Excel, and PowerPoint, and now she's working with AI as a jiu-jitsu gym manager.

The tools change, but my love of "learning something new and making work a little easier" doesn't. Whatever tool comes next, I'm sure I'll look it up, try it, and get hooked all over again.

If you're someone who says, "I like computers, but AI is still a bit...", please give it a try once. The experience you've built with Excel and Word will definitely pay off with AI too.
