10 tasks a day, 2-3 an hour: I counted how much I give Claude at once

2026.10.17 Ai Murata (Manager)

Hi, this is Ai Murata.

I do most of my office work together with Claude. Invoices, bank transfer lists, live results pages for tournaments, print files for stickers and signs, redoing award certificates. Before I knew it, "can you do this too?" and "and that one?" had become normal, and I was always asking for several things at once.

So how many things do I actually have running at any one time? I got curious and had Claude count through its own work logs. The logs covered the 65 days from August 12 to October 17, which came to 484 conversations.

When I counted Claude's logs, I was asking for 10 tasks a day on average, and 28 on my busiest day. Within an hour I usually had 2-3 going at the same time, and at the peak I had 11 running in parallel in a single hour.

Suddenly, several things are running

I never decided to start working in parallel.

When you ask for one thing, you get a little wait while Claude works on it. During that wait you think, "oh right, there's that other thing too," and you ask for it. When a reply comes back, you check it and ask for the next thing. Keep doing that and you naturally end up with several tasks running at once.

With a person, I'd hold back: "I've already asked them for that other thing, so this can wait." With Claude, there's no need for that. I can ask whenever something comes to mind, in whatever order it comes. I think that's the biggest difference.

"It does everything while I'm away" wasn't quite right

Before counting, I actually pictured it like this: while I'm away from my desk, Claude is clearing everything off my plate.

But the logs showed that most of the parallel work happened while I was at my desk. During stretches of 30 minutes or more when I wasn't talking to Claude, it was almost always working on just one thing at a time. That's because when Claude finishes a task, it stops and waits for me to check it.

In other words, what pushed the number up was my own pace of asking. Claude wasn't adding work on its own. The habit of asking for the next thing during the wait is exactly what creates the parallel work.

Summary

  • 10 tasks a day, 2-3 at once per hour is my normal pace
  • The only trick to working in parallel: ask for the next thing while you wait
  • Not everything moves forward while you're away. If something needs a check, reply when you get back

When people hear "giving work to AI," they might picture handing over one big job. For me, it's a steady stream of small jobs, with several going at the same time.
