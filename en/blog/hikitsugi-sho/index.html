My friend made a birth-leave handover guide with Claude

2026.10.18 Ai Murata (Manager)

Hi, this is Ai Murata.

Today's story is the happiest thing that's happened to me since I started this blog.

A little while ago, a friend sent me a message. "Your blog is so fun, I read the whole thing in one sitting!" and "Ichitaro, so nostalgic." (Ichitaro is an old Japanese word processing app.) She'd read all the way back to my word processor story, and she finished with, "Now I want to try Claude too!"

That alone made me really happy, but there was more.

"I made a handover guide for my husband with Claude"

Some time later, I replied to her Story with "You've got this!" and got this back.

  • Oh yeah, I tried Claude! I actually used it to make a handover guide for my husband for while I'm giving birth
  • This gorgeous document came together in no time, and it covered stuff I probably would've missed if I'd done it myself. I was like, wooow! Totally moved
  • I got the house and the kids handed over safely, with no hiccups, and I'm so grateful

My friend was about to have her third baby. She's the one who normally keeps the house and the kids running. While she was in the hospital, she'd have to hand all of it to her husband.

She made that handover guide together with Claude.

It even had the items she would have missed

What made me happiest were her words: it covered things she probably would have missed if she'd done it herself.

The things you do every day are the ones that slip through when you try to write them down. Your body remembers them, so you never need to think about them. Trash days, driving kids to lessons, things to bring, notes to the school, the usual doctor. To you they're obvious, but to the person taking over, it's all new.

Claude is great at digging out these "obvious" things. Just say "I want to hand over the house to my husband while I'm in the hospital giving birth," and it'll ask things like "What's the kids' morning routine?" and "How do you contact the school and daycare?" It helps you pick up the things you're likely to forget.

One more thing. I think nothing was missed because my friend properly explained her own household. Claude listens, organizes, and gives it shape, but only she knows what's actually in there. That's why it came out good enough to really hand over.

Useful even when it's not work

On this blog I've written a lot about using Claude at work, for things like invoices and running tournaments. But I've always wanted to get across that you can use it in everyday life, too.

I think a handover guide is the perfect example. Having a baby, a hospital stay, a business trip, a visit to your parents. Everyone has times when they leave home and need someone else to take over.

If you want to copy this, try asking like this

  • "I'll be in the hospital for a week starting next week to have a baby. I'd like to hand over the house and the kids to my husband during that time."
  • "The kids are X and Y years old. A typical weekday goes like this (just write it the way you'd say it)."
  • "If it looks like anything's missing, ask me."
  • "At the end, put it all together as a handover guide my husband can understand at a glance."

The key is not trying to write it perfectly from the start. Talk through whatever comes to mind, and have Claude ask about anything missing. That alone gets you something with far fewer gaps than writing it by yourself.
