The day I asked Claude, "Is PHP okay?"
What's happening to sites worldwide

2026.10.22 Ai Murata (Manager)

Last night I saw a headline in the news: "Serious vulnerabilities in PHP." I don't really even know what PHP is, but it made me uneasy, so I asked Claude just one short question.

  • is php ok?

Claude then checked all the files on this site (jiujitsu.co.jp) and answered like this.

This site doesn't use PHP at all, so you're fine.

Relieved, I went on to ask, "what's going on out there?" Today I'll sum up what it told me, in my own words.

So what is PHP, anyway?

PHP is a programming language for running websites. WordPress, which is common for blogs and shop sites, runs on PHP too. Apparently a huge number of sites around the world use PHP behind the scenes.

That means a weakness in PHP puts sites all over the world at risk at the same time.

What happened

Two vulnerabilities were found. Together they're called "PHP2Shell."

Severity9.2Out of 10
Vulnerabilities found2Together, "PHP2Shell"

If abused, a stranger who hasn't even logged in can run any program they like on someone else's server. In shop terms, it's like someone with no key coming in through the back door and operating the register.

  1. October 20The PHP development team announces a "serious vulnerability"Details were kept under wraps until a fix was ready. But that same day, the details leaked.
  2. October 21Companies in Japan rush to respondSakura Internet shut off features of some services, and Cygames, pixiv, and others announced emergency maintenance.
  3. October 21, nightThe fixed versions are releasedBecause of the leak, they came out one day earlier than planned, at around 22:40 Japan time.

There are also reports of attack attempts starting to appear. But as of the morning of October 22, it seems no successful takeovers have been confirmed yet. The explanations differ a little depending on the company researching it, so I'll keep watching for updates too.

Why our site was okay

This site is built to simply show pages that are already finished (apparently that's called a "static site"). It doesn't run a program on a server every time someone visits. So it had nothing to do with the PHP vulnerabilities.

According to Claude, the letters "php" do appear on the site. But only in link addresses, like the Facebook share button and the links to each gym's site in the gym guide. Any PHP running there is on their servers, not on our site.

The automatic news and search updates also run on a different system, not PHP, so they weren't affected either.

What site owners should check

We were okay, but I'm sure lots of gyms and shops have their own sites. Here's what to check, as far as I was told.

  • Do you use WordPress? If you do, you're using PHP too
  • Has your hosting or web company sent a notice? In most cases, they're the ones who fix the PHP on the server. Check your email and your admin panel notices
  • If you manage your own server, upgrade PHP to a fixed version. The fixed versions are 8.5.12, 8.4.27, 8.3.36, and 8.2.35
  • Updating WordPress or plugins alone won't fix it. This is the most important point. What needs fixing is PHP itself, running underneath

If you don't know whether your site uses PHP, the quickest way is to ask whoever built it or your hosting company. Asking Claude like I did is another option.

Summary

  • Two 9.2-severity vulnerabilities were found in PHP, which runs websites
  • Fixed versions are already out. The only fix is upgrading PHP itself
  • jiujitsu.co.jp doesn't use PHP, so it's not affected

All I did was type one simple line, "is php ok?", and Claude checked the whole site and even told me what was going on in the world. It's reassuring to have someone I can ask about things I don't understand, without having to understand them first.
