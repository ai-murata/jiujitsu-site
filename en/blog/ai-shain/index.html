Our company already had 6 AI staff

2026.09.29 · Ai Murata (Manager)

Hi, it’s Ai Murata. For post number 6, I want to talk about AI staff.

It started because it sounded cool

You hear about “AI agents” a lot lately: giving AI work the way you would an employee. To be honest, my first motive was just “That sounds kind of cool. I want that too.”

So I asked Claude, "What do you think about creating AI staff?" The answer was not what I expected.

It turns out we were already halfway there.

Making the daily to-do list every morning, prepping for payroll each month, checking membership fee payments. These jobs I’d been asking for with “Do this every month” were already running on their own on set days. I’d thought of them as just “automatic tasks.” But look at it another way, and we already had staff on the job.

I made a roster, and there were 6

So I had each one given a name and put on a roster. It looks like black and gold fighter intro cards.

  • No.001Office Clerk Checks every morning that the monthly routine jobs are done. Joined today.
  • No.002Morning Secretary Looks at my calendar, email, and chats, and sums up what to do today.
  • No.003Payroll Clerk Prepares the monthly attendance and commission sheets.
  • No.004Fee Collector Checks each month that membership fees were actually collected.
  • No.005Live Results Clerk Comes in only on tournament days and posts match times on the site.
  • No.006Deadline Keeper Comes in only on dates I can’t afford to forget, like cancellations and renewals.
  • No.007Ryozo Notes Clerk Stopped working properly and is now on leave.
  • No.008Site Watcher Patrols this site. Hiring soon.

I have to admit it’s a little funny that one of them is on leave.

One job per person

Then Claude told me something that made a lot of sense: it’s better to split roles by job than to make one “does everything” AI. There were 4 reasons.

  • You can see what’s being done and what isn’t. With a jack-of-all-trades, you lose track of “Wait, was it handling that?”
  • If one trips, the others keep going. When the Ryozo Notes Clerk really did stop, the rest of the staff carried on as usual.
  • You can set limits role by role. The money clerks don’t get to press send or pay buttons. You can draw that line for each one.
  • When you fix something, you only touch that one role. If a cutoff date changes, you just fix that role’s steps and you’re done.

It’s just like a human workplace. If you leave everything to the one person who can do it all, nobody knows anything when that person takes a day off.

Our 4 company rules

I also set rules for all the AI staff to follow.

AI Staff Rules

  • One job per person
  • Report in one place only: the to-do list
  • I press the final send, pay, and delete buttons
  • Don’t touch the screen of the computer I’m using without asking

The third one matters most to me. As I wrote in my last post about invoices, AI prepares things fast and accurately, and I do the final check. That’s the setup I trust most.

The first new hire: Office Clerk

Today I hired one new “Office Clerk.” Its job is to check every morning that the monthly routine jobs are done.

Invoices at the start of the month, transfer lists on the 10th and 25th, payroll documents by the 15th. Deadlines like these keep coming every month. If something isn’t done, the Office Clerk puts it at the very top of my to-do list. If it is done, it says nothing.

It’s not glamorous, but having it get rid of “Wait, did I do that?” is a real help.

The cool part comes later

What I learned from doing this is that “AI staff” is a name for how you present it. Inside, it’s the same automatic jobs I’d been steadily setting up.

But just by giving them names and making a roster, I can see at a glance who’s doing what. “I want it because it’s cool” turned out to be a pretty good way in.

Next I plan to hire a “Site Watcher” to patrol this site. I’ll decide after watching how the Office Clerk does for a week or two.

If you think “AI agents sound hard,” start by picking one job you repeat every month, and make a role just for that. Watching your staff grow is actually pretty fun.
