6 minutes of talking: the 14 jobs I gave Claude today

2026.10.01 Ai Murata (Manager)

Hi, this is Ai Murata. Post number 8 is a record of one day.

I once wrote that voice input is 5 times faster than typing on a keyboard. But that was just how it felt. So today, I had Claude count up one day's records.

Today's report card

Amount spokenAbout 1,800 charactersAbout 4.5 Japanese manuscript pages
Time spokenAbout 6 minutesAt 300 characters a minute
Actions by ClaudeAbout 300Opening, looking up, fixing, making
Tasks done143 of them while I slept

6 minutes of talking, 14 tasks. In writing, that's a bit less than one post on this blog.

And I'm not even speaking properly

Reading the records back, I had to laugh at myself. Here's what I actually sent Claude. (The mix-ups come from voice input mishearing my Japanese.)

  • "I'd like you to fix the schedule for the sweep in the two cambers"→ I meant the SWEEP (gym) schedules, the two in Canva
  • "Ryo-uzo, Ryo-uzo, you know, the company's representative"→ Ryozo. I said it twice and it still came out wrong
  • "The phone document (denwasho), well, selling it, let's talk about that next time"→ I meant the proposal (teiansho)
  • "Can I save it, zo please. As an image please"→ You can save it. Please send it as an image

There are tons of "um"s, "uh"s, and "well"s in there too. Even so, all 14 got through without Claude asking me to repeat myself. It properly picks up the meaning from the flow around them.

Morning: 3 tasks while I slept

Before I woke up, AI employees who run at set times had already finished 3 tasks.

  • Making today's to-do list. It even caught that an order was going to be canceled today. That was close
  • Keeping watch that the monthly routine jobs were properly finished
  • Checking the preparations for moving the shift schedule's URL

For these 3, I spoke 0 characters.

Evening: one at a time, bang, bang

  • 16:50Change "matto" back to "MAT" on the live results?I asked, and it said "better to change it back," with reasons. It made the fix and published it too.
  • 16:53A 33-minute recording was totally silentI handed over audio of Ryozo talking that I'd recorded in the car, and got back, "The content is completely zero." Maybe the car's Bluetooth took over the mic. That was the biggest shock of the day. From now on I'll turn off Bluetooth when I record.
  • 17:05"I'm stopping the LINE notifications for site updates"That was the whole message. One task done in 15 Japanese characters.
  • 19:41"I want to see the traffic"In 9 Japanese characters, I found out September had 140 people and 690 views.

Night: 6 tasks at once in 1 hour 40 minutes

This is what I most want to tell you. From 19:57 to 21:34 that night, I was moving these 6 tasks forward at the same time.

The 6 tasks running at once

  • Drafting invoice emails to clients
  • Putting a sample recipe book in my portfolio
  • Talking through also posting this blog on note (a Japanese blogging platform)
  • Planning the resident tax payment and making a tournament poster
  • Remaking the gym's schedule and flyer in Canva
  • Pulling company money records out of Box and email

It's simple. Ask for one thing, move to the next screen while Claude works, and talk again. When you go back, the answer is ready. You just keep going around like that.

When you ask a person, everything stops at the "wait" in "ask, wait, check." But you can start the next job while you wait, so the 6 tasks fit into 1 hour 40 minutes. It felt like walking around calling out to 6 staff members in turn.

A recipe book I can't release, shown with new contents

Out of the 6, this one was kind of fun. I'm turning the paper recipes from my cooking class into a recipe book you can search by ingredient on your phone. But what happens to it next will be decided together with my teacher, so the real one can't go anywhere yet.

So I made a "display version" with the same system, swapping the recipes for 12 common home-style dishes, and put it in my portfolio.

At first the photo frames just had a flower icon, which looked much lonelier than I expected. I had AI make photos for all 12 dishes too (karaage fried chicken, dashimaki rolled omelet, tonjiru pork miso soup, nikujaga meat and potato stew...), and it finally looked the part. Even for a sample, photos matter.

I fixed the schedule 3 times

I fixed the gym's schedule 3 times, just talking. "About the text size..." "No, isn't that hard to read?" "Actually, let's make it one row per hour."

Do this to a designer and you'd probably get a dirty look. Claude fixed it right away every time, and in the end it even swapped out the top half of the flyer.

Some things didn't work out

With the invoice emails, the feature that connects Chrome and Claude never did connect, and the job stopped partway through.

It still wasn't a problem, because the report when it stopped was so clear: "I haven't changed anything yet." "If you do it yourself, it's these 3 spots." You can see at a glance how far it got and where it stopped. Stopping well is another reason I feel safe delegating.

How big is the difference in the same time?

If I'd done today's 14 tasks by hand, I think it would have taken 2 full days. Checking recordings, pulling out records, remaking tables. Any one of those could easily eat half a day.

All I did was talk for 6 minutes
and check the results.

There are 2 tricks.

  • Don't try to speak neatly. Even "Ryo-uzo" gets through
  • Don't wait on each task. Ask, then move on. You can check when you come back

To start, today, take one thing that comes to mind and just say it to Claude. It's fine if you misspeak. I got the name of my own company's representative wrong twice.
